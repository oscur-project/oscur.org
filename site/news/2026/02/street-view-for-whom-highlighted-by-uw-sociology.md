title: "Street View for Whom? highlighted by UW Sociology"
date: 2026-02-10
excerpt: "A UW study of four US cities finds that how recently Google Street View imagery was captured follows socioeconomic lines, and releases GSVantage to measure it."
---

UW Sociology highlighted “Street View for Whom? An Initial Examination of Google Street View’s Urban Coverage and Socioeconomic Indicators in the US,” presented at the ACM SIGSPATIAL GeoIndustry workshop in November 2025 by Zeyu Wang, Yingchao Jian, Adam Visokay, Don MacKenzie and Jon Froehlich.

Street-level imagery from services like Google Street View has become foundational to urban informatics, from urban planning and infrastructure monitoring to neighborhood audits and real estate analytics, yet how recently it is updated varies from place to place. The authors built a pipeline that correlates the staleness of Street View imagery with census indicators such as median income and population density, and found statistically significant disparities: higher-income, higher-value suburbs tend to have older imagery than denser, more pedestrian-oriented areas. Bias like this can propagate into analyses that rely on street-level imagery instead of in-person audits.

The team also released [GSVantage](/software/#gsvantage), an interactive dashboard that maps staleness, compares it with demographics, and extends to other cities.
